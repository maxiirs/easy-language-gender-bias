# Related Works

## Use of AI in the context of simple language
AI is already being applied to various tasks in the context of simple language. For example, \citet{souayed-etal-2025-template} created a prompting framework to generate accessible images from simplified texts. In their approach, a simplified text is placed into a specific prompt template that includes accessibility constraints and style descriptions and is then fed into a text-to-image model. \citet{liu-etal-2025-document} introduced a multimodal system that enables the simplification of documents as a whole, including text simplification and the generation of supporting visuals. GPT-4o is used to simplify the source text, re-segment the original text into single-topic units, and create visual scene descriptions from these units, which serve as input for text-to-image models.
\citet{easy-language-translation} created \textit{ELGEPA}, a parallel corpus compiled by scraping the official websites of several German federal states and their capital cities, to fine-tune different Llama 3 models for automatic translation from standard German into German Easy Language. German public authorities are obligated to publish new articles in an accessible format, although these articles differ in content. Most articles deal with help, tips, news, or regional topics. The dataset contains 609 documents from eight websites, with most documents extracted from Hannover.de (272).

## Stereotypes in AI-generated Texts and Images
Stereotypes in AI-generated content is a wide and known problem but needs more focus to establish a more consious handling of such generative tools. \citet{friedrich-etal-2025-multilingual} addresses this issue by investigating gender stereotypes in images generated by multiligual text-to-image models across different languages, revealing substantial gender bias and extreme inconsistencies across 9 languages. The authors also demonstrated that simple prompt engineering is largely ineffective for bias mitigation and can actively harm text-to-image alignment.
Evaluating social bias in modern language models is crucial but challenging, particularly because existing methods struggle with flexible open-text generation scenarios. \citet{fan-etal-2024-biasalert} address this gap by introducing BiasAlert, a plug-and-play tool designed to reliably detect social biases in open-ended LLM outputs by integrating an external human knowledge retrieval database. The authors demonstrate that this retrieval-augmented approach significantly outperforms existing detection baselines and proves highly effective for both fairness evaluation and real-time bias mitigation during deployment.
